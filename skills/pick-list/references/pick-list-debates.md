# Where strong teams disagree about pick lists

Present these as choices, not as settled answers. When a recommendation takes a side, say which side and why, so the team can change it.

## Pick by rank or by data

- By rank: higher-ranked teams have shown they can win matches, and rank is what everyone else in the room sees. Simple and hard to argue with.
- By data: rank depends heavily on schedule luck (strong or weak partners, ranking-point bonuses earned by others). Scouted scoring and reliability predict playoff performance better.
- Most experienced teams lean toward data and use rank only to predict who the other captains will pick.

## How much past results count

- Pedigree: a team with a strong history (earlier events this season, past seasons) often recovers from a bad day and peaks in playoffs.
- This event only: robots change between events and seasons, and a bad qualification run may reflect a real problem.
- A common middle path: use this event's data to rank, and pedigree to break ties or to give a struggling strong team a second look on video.

## One list or two

- One list: a single order, reused for both picks. Simple, but it mixes "best scorer" with "best fit for our alliance".
- Two lists: a first-pick list built for raw strength or the role the alliance most needs, and a separate second-pick list built for reliability and complementary roles among teams likely to be left. Most strong teams build two.

## How deep the list goes

- A deep list (every team ranked, including likely backups) means the team is never caught without an answer during selection or when a backup robot is needed.
- A short list (only realistic picks) keeps the meeting focused.
- Most teams rank deeper than their expected pick position, because selection rarely goes as predicted.

## What to value in a second pick

- Reliability (moves every match, rarely breaks, never draws fouls) versus ceiling (scores a lot when it works).
- Defense: valuable in some games, but only scouts can judge it. TBA's DPR does not measure it.
- Fit: a second pick should fill the role the first two robots leave open, for example the endgame or a game piece the others can't reach.

## Do-not-pick

Whether a team belongs on a do-not-pick list is the user's decision (fouls, unsafe play, unreliability seen by their scouts). Never fill it in for them.
